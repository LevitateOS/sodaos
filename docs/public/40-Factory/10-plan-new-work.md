# Plan new work

Start with the outcome the team wants, then explore alternatives and decide what should be delivered before generating native repository issues. A saved build request preserves its direction and decisions so the team can return to it without treating a draft or an uncertain submission as accepted work.

## Describe the objective

Write the outcome in terms a teammate can assess: who needs what, why it matters and what observable behavior would satisfy the request. Add private department context only when it helps explain the request; that context belongs to its author and is not a repository policy or shared team instruction.

The Product Lead can explore alternatives, compare tradeoffs and consolidate the experience into a recommended direction. A useful exploration preserves material questions, the evidence behind recommendations and choices attributed to their actual decision makers. Factual questions may be answered from accepted requirements or accessible repository evidence. A product choice, scope change or waiver remains with the authorized human decision owner. Silence, confidence in a recommendation or a worker's answer does not authorize a choice.

Review the result before moving on. Confirm the intended audience, boundaries, success conditions and unresolved questions. If an answer changes the requested behavior or its authority, record the decision and revise the direction before generating work.

## Choose delivery and a repository

Select a delivery approach that fits the outcome. Associate the request with a repository only after the direction is clear enough to assess against that codebase. The repository must be configured for Factory operation and its Project must meet preparation requirements. Setup and ongoing repository policy belong to [Factory authorization](20-factory-authorization.md); preparing its shared environment belongs to [Prepare a Project](../50-Spaces-and-Projects/30-prepare-project.md).

Generation proposes native issues, acceptance outcomes and dependency relationships within a finite planning allowance. Read the proposed work and its native readback: issue titles and bodies, outcome coverage, dependencies, and any unresolved material decision. The native forge remains the editable source of issue content and relationships. Soda records which exact revisions and decisions were accepted.

## Confirm what was created

A successful response reports the issues and relationships observed in the repository. If submission times out or the reply is uncertain, inspect the original request and its native effects before trying again. This avoids duplicate issues when the first operation succeeded but its response did not arrive.

Generated work can proceed only when a currently code-write-authorized maintainer has accepted the objective or explicitly adopted it. Later material edits or changed dependencies require renewed acceptance and readiness assessment. An issue being present, reopened, labeled, or merely commented on does not by itself authorize Factory work. For how accepted issues become executable plans and how dependencies are reconsidered, see [Issue work and dependencies](30-issue-work-and-dependencies.md).
