# Plan new work

Describe the outcome, resolve material questions and choose an approach before associating the request with a repository and generating native work.

## Describe the objective

Write the outcome in terms a teammate can assess: who needs what, why it matters and what observable behavior would satisfy the request. Add private department context only when it helps explain the request; that context belongs to its author and is not a repository policy or shared team instruction.

Save a direction that needs more work as a draft and return to revise it later. A saved request records its current alternatives and decisions; it does not create accepted issues. If a submission response is uncertain, inspect the original request and native effects before trying again.

The Product Lead can explore alternatives, compare tradeoffs and consolidate the experience into a recommended direction. A useful exploration preserves material questions, the evidence behind recommendations and choices attributed to their actual decision makers. Factual questions may be answered from accepted requirements or accessible repository evidence.

A product choice, scope change or waiver remains with the authorized human decision owner. Silence, confidence in a recommendation or a worker's answer does not authorize a choice.

Review the result before moving on. Confirm the intended audience, boundaries, success conditions and unresolved questions. If an answer changes the requested behavior or its authority, record the decision and revise the direction before generating work.

1. Select a delivery approach that fits the outcome. Associate the request with a repository only after its direction is clear enough to assess against that codebase. The repository must be configured for Factory operation and its Project must meet preparation requirements. Setup and ongoing repository policy belong to [Factory authorization](20-factory-authorization.md); preparing its shared environment belongs to [Prepare a Project](../50-Spaces-and-Projects/30-prepare-project.md).
2. Generate work within the finite planning allowance. Read the proposed issues and their native readback: titles and bodies, outcome coverage, dependencies and unresolved material decisions. The native forge remains the editable source of issue content and relationships; Soda records which exact revisions and decisions were accepted.

## Confirm what was created

1. Confirm the issues and dependency relationships reported by the native readback. If submission times out or the reply is uncertain, inspect the original request and its native effects before trying again; the first operation may have succeeded without returning its response.
2. Ensure a currently code-write-authorized maintainer has accepted the objective or explicitly adopted it. Later material edits or changed dependencies require renewed acceptance and readiness assessment. An issue being present, reopened, labeled or merely commented on does not by itself authorize Factory work. For how accepted issues become executable plans and dependencies are reconsidered, see [Issue work and dependencies](30-issue-work-and-dependencies.md).
