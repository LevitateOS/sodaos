# Total maintenance result of library integrations

Keep the selected engines and simplify their actual callers. The strongest
remaining costs come from copied representations, parse/serialize round trips,
broad feature/dependency edges and interfaces preserved from retired code.
Libraries have removed real owned algorithms and protocols; this does not make
all remaining wrappers necessary or complete the surrounding correctness work.
The source supports targeted cuts, not a blanket rollback or a measured verdict
that the whole adoption has reduced total maintenance effort.

## What is counted and what a recommendation means

All **73 map entries** receive one primary recommendation: **62 retain** and
**11 simplify callers**. They comprise 22 crypto/wire/standard entries, 18 native
entries and 33 Go/browser/support entries. They are not 73 replacements: some
libraries predate adoption, and type declarations, tools and one indirect-module
set are support costs. Multiple entries can refer to the same cut; eleven rows
do not mean eleven independently implementable packets. Six local application
joins below expose costs that cross those entries. Optional/unadmitted candidates
have separate decisions and are not counted as completed integrations.

- **Retain** keeps the selected engine and evidenced boundary, including required
  policy. It does not approve every existing wrapper or close correctness findings.
- **Simplify callers** retains the library while removing a demonstrated bridge,
  conversion, profile emulator or unnecessary direct dependency/feature request.
- **Consolidate equivalent mechanics** needs the same producer, admission,
  authority and lifetime contract; names or similar code alone are insufficient.
- **Reconsider the library** needs a demonstrated mismatch with a required caller
  contract. A failed optional replacement proof holds that replacement only.
- **Defer an optional feature** avoids buying its integration cost before its
  benefit and prerequisites are established; required behavior remains required.

Each row distinguishes recorded deletion/transfer from current upstream
capability. No extra deletion is claimed for an already-used library. Moved
machinery, wrappers, tests, dependency/toolchain edges and operational work count
against a reduction claim. LOC is supporting context only: no uniform historical
LOC comparison, per-target linked-size measurement, latency benchmark or developer
maintenance-time measurement was performed here. Those are not prerequisites for
a source-supported narrow cut and do not justify another full build.
